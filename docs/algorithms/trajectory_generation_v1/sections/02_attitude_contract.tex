\chapter{Attitude Representation and Input Contract}

\section{Canonical Quaternions}

Runtime trajectory configuration is converted to the passive quaternion
$\quat_b^e$, which satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}^{e}=\quat_b^e\otimes\mathbf{x}^{b}.
\label{eq:q_b2e_meaning}
\end{equation}
The dynamic integrator stores $\quat_b^i$. The two are related by
\begin{align}
\quat_b^i &= \quat_e^i\otimes\quat_b^e,\\
\quat_b^e &= \quat_i^e\otimes\quat_b^i.
\label{eq:attitude_eci_ecef}
\end{align}

Quaternions are serialized scalar first:
\begin{equation}
\quat=
\begin{bmatrix}q_w&q_x&q_y&q_z\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
\end{equation}
Every accepted quaternion must be finite and nonzero. It is normalized, and
the equivalent representation with $q_w\geq 0$ is selected. A DCM must be
finite, proper, and orthonormal:
\begin{equation}
C^{T}C=\I,\qquad \det C=+1,
\label{eq:dcm_validity}
\end{equation}
within the implementation tolerance.

\section{Aerospace Roll, Pitch, and Yaw}

For every supported start-to-end frame pair, the JSON vector is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{rpy}_a^b
=
\begin{bmatrix}\phi&\theta&\psi\end{bmatrix}^{T},
\end{equation}
where $\phi$, $\theta$, and $\psi$ are roll, pitch, and yaw. NavKit uses the
3-2-1 yaw-pitch-roll composition
\begin{equation}
C_a^b
=R_3(\psi)R_2(\theta)R_1(\phi).
\label{eq:rpy_321}
\end{equation}
The payload order remains roll, pitch, yaw even though the matrix product is
written yaw, pitch, roll. The Euler angles of
$C_b^a=\left(C_a^b\right)^{T}$ are not, in
general, obtained by negating the three input values component by component.

\section{Supported Runtime Inputs}

Exactly one of the following 18 keys is required:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Direction & Quaternion & DCM & RPY \\
\midrule
body to ECEF &
\texttt{q\_b2e} & \texttt{dcm\_b2e} & \texttt{rpy\_b2e\_deg} \\
ECEF to body &
\texttt{q\_e2b} & \texttt{dcm\_e2b} & \texttt{rpy\_e2b\_deg} \\
body to ECI &
\texttt{q\_b2i} & \texttt{dcm\_b2i} & \texttt{rpy\_b2i\_deg} \\
ECI to body &
\texttt{q\_i2b} & \texttt{dcm\_i2b} & \texttt{rpy\_i2b\_deg} \\
body to NED &
\texttt{q\_b2n} & \texttt{dcm\_b2n} & \texttt{rpy\_b2n\_deg} \\
NED to body &
\texttt{q\_n2b} & \texttt{dcm\_n2b} & \texttt{rpy\_n2b\_deg} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Quaternion arrays have four scalar-first entries, DCM arrays have nine
row-major entries, and RPY arrays have three entries in degrees. Runtime input
is converted once to radians before the equations in this document are used. Reverse
directions are inverted with the quaternion conjugate or DCM transpose.

After representation conversion and direction inversion, the canonical
conversion is
\begin{align}
\quat_b^e
&=\quat_{b,\mathrm{input}}^e,
&&\text{ECEF input},\\
\quat_b^e
&=\quat_n^e(\pb^{e})\otimes\quat_b^n,
&&\text{NED input},\\
\quat_b^e
&=\quat_i^e(t,t_0)\otimes\quat_b^i,
&&\text{ECI input}.
\label{eq:attitude_input_conversion}
\end{align}
NED input therefore requires a valid, noncentral ECEF position. ECI input
requires valid, compatible $t$ and $t_0$ timestamps.

\begin{warningbox}
The accepted JSON representation is flexible; the internal convention is not.
All downstream consumers receive a normalized $\quat_b^e$, and dynamic
propagation uses $\quat_b^i$. Frame direction is always encoded in the key.
\end{warningbox}
