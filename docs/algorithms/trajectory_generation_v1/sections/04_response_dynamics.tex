\chapter{Autopilot and Vehicle Response}

\section{Actual-IMU Angular-Rate Observation}

Autopilot observes the actual configured simulated-IMU increments published by
the application. It does not instantiate a second controller-only IMU and does
not finite-difference noisy attitude. For a moving window $\mathcal{W}_k$ of
the most recent increments, the interval-average measured body rate is
\begin{equation}
\overline{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{ib,k}^{b}
=
\frac{
  \displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{W}_k}
  \Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{ib,\ell}^{b}
}{
  \displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{W}_k}
  \Delta t_{\ell}
}.
\label{eq:imu_moving_average_rate}
\end{equation}
The fixed-capacity buffer maintains both rolling sums and removes the oldest
increment and its matching duration when the selected window is full. Dividing
by the accumulated duration, rather than by a nominal sample count and period,
preserves the correct rate under exact rational timestamp spacing.

Before the first valid window sample, the current controller-state body rate is
the initialization fallback. Optional filtering before Navigator ingestion is
a different product-core design decision and is outside this trajectory
contract.

\section{Exact First-Order Step}

For scalar state $x$, constant command $u$ over one interval, and time
constant $\tau>0$, every current first-order response uses the exact
zero-order-hold solution
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1}
=u_k+\exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta t_k}{\tau}\right)(x_k-u_k).
\label{eq:first_order_exact}
\end{equation}
The operation is applied independently by axis. Vector notation below uses
$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\tau},\Delta t)$ for that
componentwise operation. For any axis whose $\tau_j=0$,
\begin{equation}
x_{j,k+1}=u_{j,k}.
\label{eq:zero_tau}
\end{equation}
Thus zero time constant is the explicit instantaneous-response limit.
Negative or non-finite time constants are invalid.

\section{Permanent Guidance Command Filter}
\label{sec:guidance_command_filter}

Guidance produces a raw total inertial-acceleration command and a raw NED bank
command. After conversion by \cref{eq:guidance_acceleration_to_force}, a
persistent command filter independently shapes body specific force and bank:
\begin{align}
\fb_{\mathrm{gflt},ib,k}^{b}
&=
\mathcal{L}\!\left(
\fb_{\mathrm{gflt},ib,k-1}^{b},
\fb_{\mathrm{gcmd},ib,k}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{f,\mathrm{g}},
\Delta t_{\mathrm{g},k}
\right),\\
\phi_{\mathrm{gflt},k}
&=
\mathcal{L}\!\left(
\phi_{\mathrm{gflt},k-1},
\phi_{\mathrm{gcmd},k},
\tau_{\phi,\mathrm{g}},
\Delta t_{\mathrm{g},k}
\right).
\label{eq:guidance_command_filter}
\end{align}
The three entries of $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{f,\mathrm{g}}$ correspond to body
$x$, $y$, and $z$; $\tau_{\phi,\mathrm{g}}$ is the bank/roll channel. A zero
entry invokes the instantaneous bypass in \cref{eq:zero_tau} for only that
channel.

Each Guidance state may define nominal filter constants that override the
trajectory-level defaults while that state is active. It may also define
entry-window constants
$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{f,\mathrm{entry},j}$,
$\tau_{\phi,\mathrm{entry},j}$, and duration $T_{\mathrm{entry},j}$. When state
$j$ is entered, those constants apply to the first sample in the new state and
remain active until the accumulated Guidance intervals reach
$T_{\mathrm{entry},j}$. The nominal constants then resume on the following
Guidance update. Applying the entry constants immediately is necessary to
shape the transition itself; delaying them would allow a zero or fast nominal
constant to pass the new command before the entry window began. Selection
precedence is therefore entry override, active-state nominal, then trajectory
default. In all cases, the filtered specific-force and bank states persist and
are never reset by a state transition or parameter change.

\section{Autopilot Attitude and Rate Loop}

When Autopilot is active and velocity alignment is well defined,
\cref{eq:banked_velocity_attitude} supplies
$\quat_{b,\mathrm{target}}^{i}$. The held target first passes through an exact
quaternion command LPF:
\begin{align}
\alpha_k
&=
\begin{cases}
1, & \tau_q=0,\\
1-\exp(-\Delta t_{\mathrm{ap},k}/\tau_q), & \tau_q>0,
\end{cases}\\
\quat_{b,\mathrm{cmd},k}^{i}
&=
\operatorname{Slerp}
\left(
\quat_{b,\mathrm{cmd},k-1}^{i},
\quat_{b,\mathrm{target},k}^{i},
\alpha_k
\right).
\label{eq:autopilot_command_lpf}
\end{align}
The deterministic command kinematics provide desired body-rate feedforward:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ff},k}^{b}
=
C_{b_{k-1,\mathrm{cmd}}}^{b_{k,c}}
\frac{
\operatorname{Log}
\left(
\left(\quat_{b,\mathrm{cmd},k-1}^{i}\right)^{-1}
\otimes
\quat_{b,\mathrm{cmd},k}^{i}
\right)
}{
\Delta t_{\mathrm{ap},k}
}.
\label{eq:autopilot_command_rate_feedforward}
\end{equation}
This is a discrete quaternion log between deterministic, filtered command
samples. It is not finite differentiation of noisy truth, navigation, attitude,
or IMU state. An analytic velocity-kinematics feedforward may replace it in a
higher-fidelity trajectory model without changing the feedback boundary.

The body-resolved attitude-error quaternion and rotation vector are
\begin{align}
\delta\quat_{\mathrm{err}}
&=
\left(\quat_{b,c}^{i}\right)^{-1}
\otimes
\quat_{b,\mathrm{cmd}}^{i},\\
\phib_{\mathrm{err}}^{b}
&=\operatorname{Log}(\delta\quat_{\mathrm{err}}),
\label{eq:trajectory_attitude_error}
\end{align}
where the subscript $c$ identifies the source-agnostic controller-facing
state, not necessarily truth.

The current proportional attitude loop with desired-rate feedback is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{cmd},ib}^{b}
=
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ff}}^{b}
+
\mathbf{K}_{\theta}
\odot
\phib_{\mathrm{err}}^{b}
+
\mathbf{K}_{\omega}
\odot
\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ff}}^{b}
-
\overline{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{ib}^{b}
\right),
\label{eq:autopilot_rate_command}
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ is componentwise multiplication,
$\mathbf{K}_{\theta}$ has units of inverse seconds, and
$\mathbf{K}_{\omega}$ is dimensionless. No integral term is present.
Autopilot's own independently
configurable body-rate response is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ap},ib,k}^{b}
=
\mathcal{L}\!\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ap},ib,k-1}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{cmd},ib,k}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\omega,\mathrm{ap}},
\Delta t_{\mathrm{ap},k}
\right).
\label{eq:autopilot_rate_response}
\end{equation}

Autopilot owns attitude tracking and body-rate command response only. It does
not own translational acceleration or specific-force dynamics. When inactive,
it retains the last well-defined/configured attitude and current body-rate
state rather than generating an artificial transient.

\section{Vehicle Internal Response and Final Limits}

The Vehicle boundary receives
$\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ap},ib}^{b}$ and the filtered body specific-force
command $\fb_{\mathrm{gflt},ib}^{b}$ from
\cref{eq:guidance_command_filter}. It owns the remaining plant response.

\subsection{Body-Rate Response}

The realized body rate has a second lag, distinct from the Autopilot response:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{raw},ib,k}^{b}
=
\mathcal{L}\!\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{ib,k-1}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{ap},ib,k}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\omega,\mathrm{veh}},
\Delta t_{\mathrm{phys},k}
\right).
\label{eq:vehicle_rate}
\end{equation}
The two angular-rate time-constant vectors therefore model separate
Autopilot/actuation and body-response bandwidths; they are not roll, pitch, and
yaw Euler-angle time constants.

\subsection{Specific-Force Response}

Both translational response stages remain inside the Vehicle boundary. An
internal command/actuator response is followed by the body response:
\begin{align}
\fb_{\mathrm{cmdrsp},ib,k}^{b}
&=
\mathcal{L}\!\left(
\fb_{\mathrm{cmdrsp},ib,k-1}^{b},
\fb_{\mathrm{cmd},ib,k}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{f,\mathrm{cmd}},
\Delta t_{\mathrm{phys},k}
\right),
\label{eq:vehicle_force_actuator}\\
\fb_{\mathrm{raw},ib,k}^{b}
&=
\mathcal{L}\!\left(
\fb_{ib,k-1}^{b},
\fb_{\mathrm{cmdrsp},ib,k}^{b},
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{f,\mathrm{rsp}},
\Delta t_{\mathrm{phys},k}
\right).
\label{eq:vehicle_force_body}
\end{align}
This preserves the agreed two-bandwidth acceleration response without moving
specific-force ownership into Autopilot.

\subsection{Post-Response Limits}

Optional limits are applied only after all first-order response stages:
\begin{align}
\omega_{ib,j,k}^{b}
&=
\clamp\!\left(
\omega_{\mathrm{raw},j,k}^{b},
-\omega_{\max,j},
\omega_{\max,j}
\right),
\label{eq:rate_limit}\\
f_{ib,j,k}^{b}
&=
\clamp\!\left(
f_{\mathrm{raw},j,k}^{b},
-f_{\max,j},
f_{\max,j}
\right).
\label{eq:force_limit}
\end{align}
Limits must be finite and strictly positive when enabled. The final limited
values are retained as the Vehicle states for the next interval and drive the
ECI plant through \cref{eq:force_to_realized_acceleration}.

\begin{warningbox}
Setting response time constants to zero makes those stages instantaneous but
does not disable configured limits. Per-axis constants and limits refer to
body $p$, $q$, $r$ and body $x$, $y$, $z$, not to NED roll, pitch, and yaw
angles.
\end{warningbox}
